\item[$\dagger$] The deployed configuration: a per-name GARCH(1,1)-$t$ Stage-1 scale, the pooled HistGBM residual-quantile body on the five features, and a pooled GPD left tail spliced at $p_0=0.025$ as the body/EVT minimum, rearranged, with no conformal overlay. A Beta-$t$-EGARCH Stage 1 was tested and not adopted: it wins on FZ0 under this frozen protocol (DM 3.31 and 4.55) and on the frozen 2000--2013 holdout (3.09 and 3.48), but under the annual refits recommended for production the two filters are indistinguishable (DM $-0.31$ and $-0.01$), so the frozen-fit gain is attributable to parameter staleness that refitting removes. Panel D reports its exception tests beside this one. 
\item Unconditional coverage is read from the date-clustered test, as the paper does elsewhere: the pooled Kupiec statistic treats every row as independent, and on a panel whose names share each day's shock the effective sample is the number of dates. The design-effect row is the ratio of the iid to the Newey--West(10) clustered variance, which is why the pooled statistic rejects on deviations of under a tenth of a percentage point. It is not an intra-date correlation. It combines clustering within a date with serial dependence across dates, and the two can be separated: on this panel the within-date component alone is 8.6 and implies an intra-date breach correlation of 0.04, while on the 2000--2013 holdout the reported 824 is a within-date component of 31, implying 0.16, multiplied by a serial-correlation factor of 25. 
\item Stage 4 is off. The committed overlay estimates its shift from the \emph{body} on the calibration block and applies it to the \emph{engine}, which already carries the EVT branch, so the tail correction enters twice; re-estimating the shift against the engine itself recovers much of that (Panel D, retargeted rows) but the shift stays negative, because the calibration window and the test window disagree in the sign of their miscoverage. A single additive out-of-era shift is the wrong instrument for a bias of under a tenth of a point, so the stage is dropped rather than repaired.
