# Comparison-fairness and leakage audit (started 2026-11-17)

Triggered by an error found in our own case study: per-instrument benchmarks were refitted every January while our
pooled parts stayed frozen, which manufactured most of GJR-skew-t's apparent off-panel lead
(`docs/CASE_STUDY_PRECOMMIT.md`, post-hoc diagnostic). Three read-only reviews then checked every comparison in the
repo against eight error classes: estimation asymmetry, information asymmetry, look-ahead, row mismatch, pooling
artifacts, inference errors, ES-convention mismatches, and code that does not do what the paper says.
Status: all three reviews are in (A frontier and decomposition jobs; B benchmark and evaluation jobs; C amortization,
case studies, tools, cross-universe). "Verified" means re-checked against code or result files after
the review; everything else is as reported and still to be checked.

## Fix runs

### H1, holdout (2026-11-17)
`HOLDOUT_STRICT=train` and `HOLDOUT_STRICT=test` on `code/paper/job_wrds_holdout.py` (frozen returns-space learner).
**Prediction, written before the run:** the top-decile edge stays positive under both variants, and its DM falls below
1.96 under at least one of them (the committed value is +0.92%, DM 2.19).

**Outcome.** The prediction held; the leak is real but small.

| run | top-decile edge | DM (one-sided p) | overall edge | pooled training rows / test rows |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| committed | +0.92% | 2.19 (0.014) | +0.04% (DM 0.4) | 414,616 / 280,608 |
| strict train (train before 2008-01-25) | +0.82% | 1.88 (0.030) | +0.02% (DM 0.2) | 396,531 / 280,608 |
| strict test (score after 2009-03-09) | +0.83% | 4.08 (<0.001) | +0.25% (DM 2.9) | 414,616 / 242,293 |

Removing the overlapping training rows costs a tenth of the edge and takes the DM from 2.19 to 1.88: significant
one-sided at 5% (the paper's stated convention for a direction fixed in advance), not two-sided. The threshold shape is
unchanged (deciles 1–8 between −0.20% and +0.08%, decile 9 +0.22%, decile 10 +0.95% by row weighting). Scoring only
dates after every training row, which drops the 2008 crisis from the test, gives the same edge with DM 4.1. The paper
should report the strict-train figure as the holdout result and say the calendar-split check was not what closed the
channel there. Files: `results/paper/diagnostics/holdout_frontier_results_strict_{train,test}.json`.

### H1, point-in-time universe (2026-11-17)
`PIT_STRICT=train` and `PIT_STRICT=test` on `code/paper/job_pit_universe.py`. Committed: overall +0.61% (DM 9.16), frozen
top bucket +3.17% (DM 7.0). **Prediction, written before the run:** under strict training the overall edge falls by more
than half (the review found it fifteen times the holdout's, the signature of same-date information), while the top
bucket stays positive with DM above 2.

**Outcome.** The prediction held, and here the overlap matters.

| run | overall edge (DM) | frozen top bucket (DM) | bottom bucket | training rows / test rows |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| committed | +0.61% (9.16) | +3.17% (7.02) | +0.42% (5.31) | 307,685 / 208,556 |
| strict train (train before 2003-04-24) | +0.04% (5.34) | +1.25% (4.06) | +0.00% | 130,977 / 208,556 |
| strict test (score after 2009-06-24) | +0.47% (4.42) | +1.77% (3.08) | +0.02% (0.18) | 307,685 / 144,899 |

The top-bucket edge survives both variants and stays significant, at 1.25% to 1.77%, not 3.17%. The overall edge and
the positive bottom bucket of the committed run do not survive strict training. Caveat: strict training also removes 57%
of the pooled training rows and every post-2003 regime, so it mixes leak removal with less and older data; strict
testing keeps all training data and drops the crisis from the test. The two bracket the honest figure. The paper's
"larger than in the survivor-tilted holdout" no longer holds: the clean point-in-time top-bucket edge is of the same
order as the holdout's. Files: `results/paper/diagnostics/pit_universe_results_strict_{train,test}.json`.

### H4 and H5, benchmark refits (2026-11-17)
`tools/r67_refit_benchmarks.py`, predictions in its header, result `results/paper/diagnostics/r67_refit_benchmarks.json`.
The script's re-run of the paper's own fitting reproduces the dumped losses exactly (GAS 2.23238 / 1.89905, Taylor
2.16472 / 1.84899, SAV-CAViaR pinball 0.39554), so the comparison below isolates the fitting.

| benchmark | level | as published | refitted (full window, more starts and iterations) | accuracy layer |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Taylor ES-CAViaR | 1% | 2.1647, engine DM 3.76 | 2.1616, engine DM **3.17** | 2.1465 |
| Taylor ES-CAViaR | 2.5% | 1.8490, DM 0.13 | 1.8493, DM 0.24 | 1.8486 |
| GAS | 1% | 2.2324, DM 9.91 | 2.2195 (scales estimated), DM 10.77 | 2.1465 |
| GAS | 2.5% | 1.8991, DM 8.78 | unstable: 489 non-finite test rows | 1.8486 |
| SAV-CAViaR, eleven-level pinball | — | 0.39554; engine DM −1.63 overall, −1.96 top decile | 0.39592; engine DM **−0.38** overall, **−0.77** top decile | 0.39603 |

**Outcome.** The descriptions in the paper were wrong; the conclusions hold.
- Taylor: the accuracy layer still wins at 1% (P2 predicted the win would fall below 1.96; it did not) and ties at 2.5%.
  All 200 fits converge. In the top score decile Taylor is ahead of the accuracy layer (DM −1.8 and −2.6).
- GAS: estimating the scales improves it by 0.013 at 1% (P1 predicted more than 0.03) and the accuracy layer still wins
  easily. At 2.5% the free-scale model diverges out of sample on some names, which is presumably why the scales were
  fixed. GAS as implemented here remains worse than GARCH-t and is a weak benchmark; the paper should say that its GAS is
  a restricted one-factor version and not lean on the comparison.
- SAV-CAViaR: a thorough optimisation fits the estimation window better and forecasts slightly worse (0.39592 against
  0.39554). Against the well-optimised CAViaR the accuracy layer ties in every region (P3 predicted a significant CAViaR
  lead; it failed). The −1.96 in the canonical file comes from the short-budget fit. "Ties SAV-CAViaR" stands; the table
  still has to be regenerated from the seeded file, and the text should state the optimiser budget.
