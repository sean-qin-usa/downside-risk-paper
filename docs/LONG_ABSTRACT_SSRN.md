# Long abstract (for SSRN / arXiv / working-paper page)
The journal abstract is capped at 100 words (JFEC); this is the full version for venues without the cap. It matches R67.

**When Do Flexible Tail-Risk Models Beat GARCH? Post-Shock Scale Error, Residual Shape, and a Real-Time Score**

Flexible tail-risk models are regularly reported to forecast Value-at-Risk and Expected Shortfall better than GARCH. We ask where that advantage is earned and why. On 200 US stocks and 221.6k test asset-days, with inference over dates, the answer to the first question is narrow: a pooled gradient-boosted model of the GARCH residual quantile beats GARCH(1,1)-t by 3.0% of pinball loss (DM 10.5) in the top decile of a trailing 63-day kurtosis of the filter's own residuals, with a smaller edge in the ninth decile and none in the other eight; the edge is larger after positive shocks than negative ones. SAV-CAViaR, which shares nothing with the learner, has the same profile. The concentration replicates on an untouched 2000–2013 holdout under a specification frozen in advance, in a point-in-time universe with delistings, under calendar splits and under annual refits.

The answer to the second question starts from what a top-decile score records. In virtually every top-decile window one residual, a median 8.2 conditional standard deviations, carries most of the window's fourth moment, and the residuals that follow it are compressed by the variance the filter carries after the shock. Most of what flexible models earn on those days is a correction to that variance. A GARCH whose response to a single shock is capped, with the Student-t tail unchanged, beats standard GARCH-t by more than the flexible model does in the top decile. A Beta-t-EGARCH, robust to a single shock by construction, beats both flexible models there. A smaller residual-shape component survives on the robust scale (+0.27%, DM 2.0, in the top decile) and lowers the joint (VaR, ES) loss at both regulatory levels under frozen fits and annual refits.

The semiparametric estimator that carries these comparisons ties the best parametric benchmarks on the joint loss rather than beating them, and the paper makes no capital claim. The practical reading is a monitor: the score marks the days on which a GARCH(1,1)-t in production is stressed, mostly through its own post-shock scale, and a rule that switches to the robust filter on those days, with the threshold fixed in advance, matches running the robust filter everywhere on the panel, though it does not beat GARCH-t on instruments outside it. The evidence is for individual US stocks: on other asset classes the estimator is comparable to the standard benchmarks at the 2.5% level and the post-shock mechanism does not appear.
